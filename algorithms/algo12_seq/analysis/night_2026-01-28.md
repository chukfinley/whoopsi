**algo5 (misses entirely):** its 4-class head reads the irregular elevated HR as REM and labels the whole block REM (algo5 block dist = [0,0,0,13]). It has no awake gate at all on this night.

**algo12 (catches 77%, leaks the middle):** the cascade is `awake = pa≥0.40; rem = (~awake) & pr≥0.40`. The **awake gate `pa` collapses in the middle** of the block (win 198/199/200: pa = 0.32 / 0.14 / 0.06) while the **REM gate stays saturated** (pr = 0.98 / 0.75 / 0.73). Because pa falls below 0.40, those windows fall through to the REM branch and are grabbed by the high pr. The misses are **not "just below threshold"** — pa is genuinely tiny (0.06–0.32). The awake gate's own features (HR-variability) momentarily look like REM (irregular HR, low movement, mid-night) and the binary awake-vs-rest model can't separate restless-awake from REM in those 3 windows. This is a **gate confusion**, not a Viterbi-smoothing erasure (the light/deep Viterbi never touches these — they're assigned awake/rem before it runs).

## 4. Root cause

Restless-awake and REM are near-degenerate in the cardiac signal (both = irregular elevated HR, low movement, mid/late night). The awake gate `pa` is locally unstable across the block and **dips below tau in a 3-window pocket where the REM gate is confidently high**. The cascade's hard "rem = not-awake AND pr≥tau" rule then converts that pocket to REM. Lowering tau_a globally or a gate-duel margin does NOT recover this pocket (pa is far below pr there) and costs light recall elsewhere. Accelerometer/movement cannot help — it is flat.

## 5. Proposed fix (tested on this night)

**Awake-bridge post-processing:** after the cascade assigns labels, relabel any run of non-awake windows of length ≤ L to awake **iff it is flanked by awake on both sides within the same night**. Rationale: a 4–6 min REM/light island wedged between awake windows is almost always a gate dropout in a continuous awake episode (people don't dip into a single REM epoch mid-arousal).

Measured on the full dataset (78 nights, oof_v2 gates), appended after `cascade.decode`:

| variant | central block recall | global Awake | Light | Deep | REM | acc | min-recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 77% | 71.1 | 71.0 | 81.9 | 70.8 | 73.26 | 70.8 |
| **bridge L=2** | 77% | **76.8** | 70.0 | 81.9 | 70.8 | 73.14 | **70.0** |
| bridge L=3 | **100%** | 79.1 | 69.3 | 81.9 | 69.9 | 72.79 | 69.3 |
| bridge L=4 | 100% | 80.2 | 68.4 | 81.8 | 68.4 | 72.05 | 68.4 |

- **L=2** is the safe pick: keeps every stage ≥70% (MIN 70.0), lifts global Awake recall +5.7pp (71.1→76.8) at −0.12pp accuracy, but does NOT recover this specific 3-window pocket (gap length 3 > 2).
- **L=3** recovers the circled block fully (**77%→100%**) and lifts global Awake to 79.1% (+8pp), at the cost of REM/Light dropping just below the 70% target (69.9 / 69.3) and −0.47pp accuracy.

Recommendation: ship **L=2** as the conservative default (all stages stay ≥70%), or **L=3** if maximizing awake recall on multi-window arousals is the priority. The deeper fix is to make the REM gate subtract awake-gate evidence (train `pr` to output low on restless-awake), but the bridge is the cheap, testable win.
